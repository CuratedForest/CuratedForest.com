As I've read over the academic literature, what's jumped out at me is
there are hormone treatments that have a positive impact on berry size.
Often at the expense of overall harvest, as well as taste and aroma,
but these are trade offs I'm happy to make (for the size experiments).
I've identified 4 hormones to start with, but expect to expand that as
I learn more.

## Which Hormones (Auxins)

- PP333
- CCC
- NAA
- CPPU

## The Protocol

I'm not only curious about which hormones produce big fruit, but also
how and when it's delivered impacts size and flavor. Because the structures setting the berry's upper
size limit are in place before the strawberry even flowers, I'd like to
understand when it actually makes sense to apply hormones.

## The Conditions

### In Tissue Culture

I'd like to start some plants in tissue culture. The idea being here I
can experiment with initial amounts of the various hormones. Then when
they grow I can see the impact of the initial hormone dose.

All plants will come from tissue culture, but with varying initial hormone amounts.

### Across Life Span

In addition to the plants started in tissue culture, I'll have a
condition where hormone treatment continues via root uptake throughout
the plant's life.

### Stop Before Flowering

Because the fruit structures are in place before flowering, we'll target stopping treatment at this point to see if
reducing the hormone load beyond this will help improve the negative flavor
impacts.

### Target Time Period Specifically

Finally, rather than applying hormones from the start, I'd like to time hormone treatment specifically to when the
size determining structures (achene) are actually being formed. This
may need to lead the flower formation by some days or weeks.

## Follow Up Experiments

Once I have an idea which hormones are effective or not, I'll explore
further the optimal dose at the given life cycle.
