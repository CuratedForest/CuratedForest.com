Below are some resources and organizations that I've come across that have been particularly helpful. If you have a resource or other contribution, please feel free to use the edit button at the top of this page and create a pull request.

# Videos
## Compressive lectures

### [Physiology of Plant Production under Controlled Environment](https://web.archive.org/web/20210724022341/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=upwQrEJ-ZLY&list=PL7fPr3CuAdvv4ZPJKPvi4U7VblxKJnegD)
Dr. Chieri Kubota recorded and posted her 400 level lectures for an entire semester. Goes fairly deep into the science driving plant growth.
She's studied strawberries, among other crops, so their physiology is talked about a decent amount.

## [Indoor Ag Science Cafe](https://www.ceaforum.org/indoor-ag-science-cafe)
This monthly meeting has great CEA related speakers & discussions.

## [Sustainable Hydroponic and Soilless Strawberry Production Systems](https://www.youtube.com/@sustainablehydro/videos)
This series covers the basics on actual strawberry production in a greenhouse. A little basic and not aeroponic, but still a good resource for someone new to the field.

## [Dr. Bruce Bugbee](https://www.youtube.com/c/Apogeeinstrumentsincorporated/videos)
He's a cannabis researcher out of Utah State (surprise I know). In addition to cannabis, he covers light extensively - he started Apogee, a light sensor company.

## [Plants in Jars](https://www.youtube.com/@plantsinjars)
While Laur isn't a professor, her content makes at home plant tissue culturing approachable and understandable.

## [High Tech Gardener](https://web.archive.org/web/20210724024200/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k6cfZga7atw&list=PLnTwGofHB4LCIjPF9oDSxV7_J_QMDvmEj)
Dee gives the best overview of what HPA (High Pressure Aeroponics) is, how to design a HPA system, and his thoughts and lessons gained from his own build. His videos helped me land on a water tight design (plastic totes). Super nerd, and I love it.

## [LED Gardener](https://www.youtube.com/@LEDGardener/videos)
His Home Assistant hydroponic water system directly inspired my water supply systems. Not a professor but a great engineer!

Originally found because his home build lighting guides taught me how to put together my first light rigs.
I ended up following [this guide](https://web.archive.org/web/20210724023858/https://ledgardener.com/diy-led-strip-build-designs-samsung-bridgelux) and using Bridgelux strips.
Note: While his YouTube channel is still online, the website is not. Thus you'll have to put up with the Wayback Machine.

## [SAG's lighting guide](https://www.reddit.com/r/HandsOnComplexity/comments/onwuws/sags_lighting_guide_cheat_sheet/)
This isn't a video, but is the best collection of information I've seen on lighting. In particular, how to convert lux to ppdf and what reasonable ranges for that are.

# Organizations

## [ADVANCEA/CEA Forms](https://www.ceaforum.org/)
They organize and put on the Indoor Ag Science Cafe, among other resources. Comprised of a handful of other CEA supporting organizations that are also worth checking out!
- [OptimIA](https://scri-optimia.org/index.php)
- [CEA Herb](https://scri-ceaherb.org/)
- [UGGA](https://greenhousegrowers.org/)

## [Agritecture](https://www.agritecture.com/)
Everywhere I look in the CEA industry, I see Agritecture pop up. Has a bit of a start up vibe, but worth checking out and potentially using for consulting if building a farm.

## [Indoor Ag-Con](https://indoor.ag/)
Every winter Indoor Ag-Con is held in Las Vegas. It's a great opportunity to network and see what's going on in the industry.
