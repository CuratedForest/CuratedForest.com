Dear -----,

My name is Spencer, and I'm writing because I'm looking for help narrowing in
on an academic focus. Your work on ----- piqued my curiosity and made me think
you might have a helpful perspective.

Ever since childhood I've been interested in how software meets the real
world, and have spent 20+ years honing my craft as a software engineer. At the
end of the day, I've learned that I want to use technology to help grow food
and feed people better. I've been leaning into this goal personally for 10
years now, and have gotten to play with Aerofarm's software and data
professionally for the last ~5 years.

My ultimate goal is to build my own farm. Obviously I want to grow and sell
food (profitably), but also am looking to advance the CEA space as it comes
out of its infancy. My belief is the fastest way to do that is with open
source software and a large community.

In the near term, my next step is graduate school, but the list of topics I'd
like to study is more than I can reasonably shove into a single program. So
I'm hoping to lay out the domains I'd be interested in and see if you have any
suggestions on which topics would be particularly impactful, as well as if you
have any suggestions on a particular focus.

## Crop Production

Outside of a specific plant, I've fallen in love with aeroponics. This high
risk high reward growing method appeals to the software engineer in me. One
game I play professionally is downtime. The way my industry thinks about it
is, for every minute of downtime how many millions of dollars are lost...
Aeroponics is all the same skills, but a *much* easier game — I've written
more about it on my {{< ref "plants/aeroponics/applying-devops" >}} page.
Ultimately I've applied these concepts and practices when building an
automatic and open source aeroponic grow & data collection system. As I'm
getting environmental parameters dialed in, what I'm finding is that when I
look for growing research, resources, and suggestions, I can find information
on how to grow in dirt, and sometimes hydroponically, but the aeroponic
baselines either haven't been studied or have been a bit hush hush. I'd love
to collect what information we do have, fill in any gaps with research and
experimentation, and then write the textbook: *How to Grow With Aeroponics*.
Or at least get started!

## Plant Biology

The plants that currently captivate my imagination are CEA strawberries. I see
so much potential for flavorful, off-season, local berries — a topic I've
written about on my {{< ref "plants/strawberries/financial-viability" >}}
page. Having investigated the process, I understand that we don't see many CEA
strawberries because they are one of the more complex crops to manage and
grow. The challenge I set for myself to guide my learning thus far has been
figuring out how to grow the world's biggest strawberry (see
{{< ref "plants/strawberries/world-record-size" >}}). It didn't work anything like I thought initially, but now I am
particularly interested in flower formation and what drives ----- to divide at
the --- stage. Figure if I can create the right environments, I can target the
ideal conditions for ------ formation when those are forming, and then shift
targets to ideal ----- filling to ultimately create the biggest berry. Once
that's figured out, I'd like to see if I can shift the hormone application to
enable large berry formation, while preserving some flavor. One approach I'm
particularly curious about is how we can use aeroponics, and the precision
irrigation it provides, to manipulate and optimize hormones across the growth
cycle.

## OSS in Ag

Coming from the software world first, I recognize the importance of being able
to modify any piece of hardware or software on my farm. The only way I know
how to do that is build it myself or use open source solutions. I'm a good
programmer, but even if I spent every minute of my life coding it wouldn't
compare to the hours other people have already invested into the free and open
solutions I run. What I've found is not only are the solutions free, but
usually have more integrations to other systems, can have better communities
for support, and are full featured rather than pay walled. But then I turn
around and look at what farmers are being sold and with a few little
exceptions, it's primarily closed. John Deere is literally the bogeyman of the
right to repair movement. In this domain I'd be curious to dig into what
software and hardware farmers are using and why, with the hope to provide
awareness around potentially better open source options. This is one place my
time in industry has greatly informed me. 5 years ago I was thinking about how
to collect plant data. Today I have that mastered and am grappling with how to
manage and collect business AND farm data. Beyond environmental conditions, a
CEA farm requires tracking for financials in an ERP, FSQA forms, safety forms,
sample testing, maintenance and calibrations; as well as systems for security,
cameras, printers, tablets, and hand scanners.

## The Ask

So clearly this is too much for one degree. What I'm looking for can be boiled
down to 3 questions.

- First, realistically, which of these makes sense to pursue for a graduate
  degree?
- Second, does exploring these lend itself more towards a master's degree or a
  PhD?
- Third, I wouldn't have reached out if I didn't think there was potential for
  collaboration, but I'm curious if you think your program would encourage
  research in some of these directions?

Any time you have for a response is deeply appreciated,

Spencer Hachmeister
